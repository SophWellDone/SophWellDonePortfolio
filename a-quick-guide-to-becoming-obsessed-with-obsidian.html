Intro

Welcome to my series of small guides where I showcase cool stuff I've learned or discovered how to do in Obsidian! If you are anything like me, you'll end up spending a lot of time creating small systems like these, justifying the title. These are what I find useful and/or fun, meaning this is not objectively the best and/or right way to do certain things in Obsidian.

What is Obsidian?

'The free and flexible app for your private thoughts.' (Obsidian website)

Obsidian is, in my own words, an incredibly versatile tool that can organise pretty much anything. To prove that, heres some usecases I personally have used it for:

  • DnD campaigns (this is a popular use!)
  • Narrative design
  • Daily journaling and tasks.
  • Organising university notes
  • Organising notes about my hobbies.
  • Creating a game library, with reviews and metadata attached.
  • Creating a Fallout: New Vegas style with a retro theme
  • Syncing all of the above between my PC, phone and Uni Workstations

Important Note: If your just starting out, I recommend not thinking too hard about this post. I don't want to overwhelm you! Just start by jotting down what you want to remember for later in a plain old note. Before long you'll start researching ways of doing things better in the way your brain likes to do them. But if you still want a guide on certain areas, you are more than welcome to keep reading :)

I'm ready to learn!

Great! I have lots of little snippets I'm more than happy to share. I recommend using the Table of Contents below to skim through and pickup what you find interesting.

I'll be using the default Obsidian theme for dark mode, other than any guides that talk about themes directly. These guides were made on Obsidian 1.14.4 for Windows 11 but should still be up-to-date. Lemme know if that changes!

For Absolute Beginners: The 'Game Library' guide is the best place to start! It assumes you have very little Obsidian and scripting experience. If you want help traversing Obisidian's settings then that one is a good starting point!

Tasks [W.I.P]

Tasks is a very important part of my experience. This plugin has transformed the way I think about my daily studying and life, everything feels a little bit more managble now.

This guide is a little more advanced and requires a community made plugin. Obsidian stores it's files as unecrypted markdown, so be careful what plugins you install and what data could be risked if one were to be malicious. However, Tasks is a very well maintained plugin with millions of downloads so I personally trust it to be safe!
